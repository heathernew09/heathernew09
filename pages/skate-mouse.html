MOUSE+ SKATE

I created this mouse concept as a fun way to connect my love for skating and my agility around a computer. You'll find it in my footer peeking out and responding to your movement around the page. Now it's become a real life thing thanks to AI, a labor day weekend and my 3d printer. Check out the process below. Yes, it rolls.

Project Goal

3d printed Rolling
Mini Quad Roller Skate

Transform the sleek, minimalist Apple Magic Mouse into a fully functional (aesthetically and mechanically) mini quad roller skate. Driven by a rapid, iterative CAD workflow using OpenSCAD for parametric precision.

KEY WINS

  • • Zero-Support Geometry
  • • Mechanical Glue-Assembly
  • • Self-Gapping Glue Nuts
  • • Flawless Fitment
Methodology

THE PROCESS

Parametric Coding: Building every component via mathematical variables, allowing us to instantly scale, slam, and stretch the design.

Subtractive Sculpting: Carved a massive 3D ellipsoid bowl out of the top of the plate to exactly cradle the mouse's curvature for a gapless, snap-in fit.

Innovation

SELF-GAPPING
GLUE NUTS

A custom hollowed hex nut caps the axle to an exact depth, automatically leaving a perfect 0.8mm gap for the wheel to spin freely.

The Evolution: Human + AI Pair Programming

The Beginning

THE First Draft

Our initial block-in of the skate geometry. Before any aggressive styling, dropping, or 3D cupping was applied, we established the basic proportions.

Modeling and refining the design

Visual Grounding

Why upload photos? Providing real-world references gives the AI instant geometric context. Instead of struggling to describe complex spatial relationships, these photographs of my actual roller derby skates grounded the AI in authentic proportions and aggressive stance profiles.

Prompt Callout: "something i noticed is the toe stop angles directly down right now... let's only look at the toe stop here. it's angled out, maybe 45degree angle not straight down..."
Scaling and repositioning

Applying the Reference

Directly applying the geometry from the skate photos, the AI iteratively pitched the toe stop boss forward and flattened the rubber wedge to match the derby profile. The base block was aggressively slimmed down into a sleek, skeletal silhouette.

Specific edits and revisions

"Printing different versions showed us where we needed to dial in the different areas. Scaling for a mouse to look exactly as I wanted was a balance between size, width, length. There is also a delicate curve to the mouse and rails to match.”

Communicating to the AI

Often the results would be as I asked, often the solutions the AI proposed would add additional hurtles. The solution was a balancing act between what I was directing and what it was interpreting. I took many screenshots and circled areas in markup to direct and clarify which section I wanted edited.

To prevent messy 3D printer supports from ruining the center cradle, we carved a massive "Skeleton Window" entirely through the block. This completely eliminated blind overhangs while saving material. Each test print I adjusted wether the skate would print wheels down or wheels up. The final print was wheels up, this gave the bottom of the plate a perfect finish.

"i can't wait to print this! i printed v13... it probably has an hour left."

Refining the design

Moving from digital to physical. These early test prints confirmed our overall geometry but immediately highlighted areas where the printer struggled with overhangs and thin walls. Supplying the AI with these images helped me establish my intent and what I wanted to improve. We were able to dial in the curves of the mouse left to right but also front to back to nest it with the black rails perfectly.

"to print in place multi colors this small thing would take 11 hours and waste a lot of filament. I envision being able to push the toe stop bolt into a hole and glue it."

The Mechanical Pivot

Rather than wasting 11 hours and spools of filament on automated AMS color swaps, we re-architected the CAD for modular glue-assembly. We engineered precise 6.4mm anchor holes, bare axles, and slip-over nuts so the skate could be assembled by hand perfectly.

Evolution

Version History

We rapidly progressed through 21 major iterations.
V1-V3 established base geometry.
V6-V8 chopped the plate down to exactly 77x36mm.
V9-V10 introduced the breakthrough "3D Cradle".
V17 brought the mechanical glue-assembly overhaul, separating parts for zero-waste printing.
V21 was our final version. It includes printing in place with swappable wide body wheels.
