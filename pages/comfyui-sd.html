ComfyUI + Stable Diffusion

Deconstructing
Diffusion

I spent some time looking under the hood of Stable Diffusion to understand exactly how AI draws. By hooking into the ComfyUI API on my local M5 Pro, I ran a series of controlled experiments to capture the internal "denoising" process at specific intervals.

Step 01

The Latent Seed

Everything starts with noise. In Stable Diffusion, we don't work with pixels immediately. Instead, we initialize a Latent Space—a compressed representation of an image. At Step 01, this is just a matrix of random numbers (Gaussian noise). The "Seed" you hear about is just the starting configuration of this randomness.

Step 10

The U-Net Loop

This is where the heavy lifting happens. A neural network called the U-Net takes that noise and the text prompt you provided. It's been trained to predict what noise needs to be subtracted from the image to move it closer to your description. By Step 10, you can see large structural forms starting to emerge from the chaos.

Step 20

Attention to Detail

As the denoising loop continues, the model moves from broad shapes to specific textures. Using a mechanism called Cross-Attention, the U-Net looks back at your prompt (e.g., "translucent orange casing") and ensures those concepts are physically manifesting in the latent representation.

Step 30

The VAE Handshake

The final step is the most critical for human eyes. All the denoising happened in a mathematical 4x8x128 latent space. To see it as a 1024x1024 image, we use the VAE Decoder. It "uncompresses" the latent math back into the colorful pixels we recognize.

The Engine

Juggernaut XL

The photorealistic quality of these generations is powered by Juggernaut XL. Built on the SDXL architecture and refined by the team at RunDiffusion, this model is specifically engineered for high-fidelity textures, lifelike skin tones, and intricate mechanical details.

Key features include GPT-4 vision-captioned datasets for better prompt adherence and a "Positive/Negative Package Merging" technique that drastically improves lighting, contrast, and atmospheric depth.

The Stack

Local AI Infrastructure

To keep this experiment entirely private and cost-effective, everything runs locally on Neo (Apple M5 Pro). This allows for rapid iteration and zero token costs, utilizing the unified memory and GPU acceleration of Apple Silicon.

  • Hardware: M5 Pro · 64GB RAM
  • Engine: ComfyUI (Local API)
  • Model: Juggernaut XL v9
  • Orchestration: Python · Custom JSON Workflows

By treating ComfyUI as a programmable backend rather than just a UI, I was able to automate the capture of internal latent states. This approach turns a generative model into a transparent educational tool, bridging the gap between high-level creative requests and low-level mathematical execution.

Engineering the Workflow

To capture these steps, I wrote a custom Python bridge that intercepts the ComfyUI generation loop. Instead of one KSampler node, I chained multiple samplers together, decoding the VAE at each checkpoint to externalize the "hidden" progress.

# Chaining KSamplers to capture intermediate latents ksampler_id = str(node_id) workflow[ksampler_id] = { "class_type": "KSamplerAdvanced", "inputs": { "start_at_step": current_step, "end_at_step": target_step, "return_with_leftover_noise": "enable" } }

This experiment proves that AI isn't "magic"—it's a series of extremely precise mathematical transformations. Understanding these layers allows for much greater control over the creative output, from ControlNet conditioning to custom LoRA training.
