The Brief

A Networking Event Like None Other

Executives have seen every conference trick. For BUILT's second year, the technology had to feel personal from the first moment, give strangers a reason and a script to start talking, stay invisible with no data leaving the room, and hold up for four days in a resort with no IT desk.

Station 1 · Arrival

Welcome Registration

Guests tap their badge, get greeted by first name, and answer five quick questions written for their role. Their answers reveal one of six archetypes, from The Spark to The Connector to The Maker.

The Logic

Getting to Know Our Attendees

We started from a base set of five questions, then rewrote them for each attendee's title and discipline, so a CMO and a chief medical officer each answered in their own language.

The archetypes aren't a scientific profile like DISC. They're math on your answers: every choice is worth two points, either to one archetype or split between two, and the highest total wins. Ties go to whichever archetype has the fewest people so far, which kept the room balanced.

It still took real thought. We wanted every result to feel like it got you, and to point you toward someone worth meeting.

Complementary Pairs

Built to Meet Each Other

We paired archetypes by personality, so opposites with something to trade found each other. Your pair carried straight into the next activation, the reception dinner.

  • The Spark ↔ The Anchor Momentum meets grounding
  • The Connector ↔ The Maker People meet things built by hand
  • The Lens ↔ The Compass New perspective meets a clear road map
Station 2 · Reception Dinner

Pour & Pair

A tap pours a wine matched to your archetype and prints a receipt: who you are, who to seek out, and two questions to ask them. Come back for another glass and it sends you to meet someone new.

Station 3 · After Dinner

Toasty Tales

At the bonfire, a tap plays a spooky campfire story over the speaker. It says your name, knows what you do, and lets a monster loose in your world. People were shocked, then they wanted to hear everyone else's.

Tailoring the Stories

Every Story Knew Your World

Before the event, I sorted every registered attendee into one of eight disciplines using their title and the event team's guest list. That bucket shaped two things: the version of the welcome quiz they saw, and the world their Toasty Tale took place in.

One quiz question did the heavy lifting: "What keeps you up at night?" Each discipline got its own version with four answers in its own language. Their answer decided what their story was about.

Process

The Decisions That Mattered

  • Prototype to persuade. I built the quiz logic as a Lovable.dev demo for internal reviews, which got the idea approved before any hardware existed.
  • From live AI to approved AI. Our first prototype wrote stories live with a local language model. In front of executives, surprises are a risk, so we generated all 216 stories ahead of time from a layered prompt built on the quiz and archetype data, then approved and edited every one for guardrails.
  • No waiting at the fire. Every story was narrated on-device before guests arrived, with whisper, pause and lean-in cues turned into real pacing.
  • Built not to fail in a field. Two peer "brains" sync every 20 seconds so either can drop out, and answers save after every question.
  • Private by design. Local networks only. No cloud, no accounts, no guest data leaving the venue.
The Fleet
  • 7 Raspberry Pis, each with a hand-soldered PN532 NFC reader
  • 2 brains: Pi 5, touchscreen, NVMe, real-time clock
  • 3 wine stations: Pi 4, touchscreen, receipt printer
  • 2 firesides: headless Pi, Sonos speaker
  • Stack: Node, SQLite, React, Vite, WebSockets
  • Voice: Kokoro-82M text-to-speech, rendered on-device
  • Live: a remote control that fired sound bites
Process · Sketch

A Scan Point on Top of the Speaker

The fireside stations had to hide a Raspberry Pi and an NFC reader without turning the Sonos Move into a gadget. I sketched an enclosure that sits on top of the speaker:

  • "Scan here" lid. The PN532 reader mounts to the underside, right under the icon, with a rim so the lid seats into the box.
  • A curved cutout. The bottom of the box arches around the speaker, so its top controls stay visible and there's room for fingers to lift it.
  • Everything inside. The Pi mounts in the box, with a single exit for the 5V power cord.
Process · 3D Print

Custom Scan Spots

A bare NFC reader is just a circuit board, so I modeled a cap for each one with the event's horseshoe mark and a clear "Scan Badge Here." Each cap prints as three parts in three colors: the body, the starburst and the lettering.

The goal: no one should have to ask where to tap.
